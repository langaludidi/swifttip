# SwiftTip — Phased Go-to-Market Strategy (GTM-0 → GTM-6)

**Companion to `16-vision.md`.** The vision governs product scope; this document
governs how SwiftTip reaches the market. To avoid clashing phase numbers, GTM
stages are numbered **GTM-0 through GTM-6** and map to vision phases as follows:

| GTM stage | Covers | Vision phase |
|---|---|---|
| GTM-0 Prepare the market | pre-pilot readiness | Phase 1 (entry) |
| GTM-1 Prove the tip | one-site pilot | Phase 1 |
| GTM-2 Prove repeatability | 3–10 sites | Phase 1 → 2 transition |
| GTM-3 Win the fuel beachhead | 10–100 sites | Phase 2 |
| GTM-4 Expand by vertical | hospitality, tourism, etc. | Phase 2–3 |
| GTM-5 Own the category | worker identity & earnings | Phase 3–4 |
| GTM-6 Infrastructure & ecosystem | APIs, white-label | Phase 5 |

**Verify-first flags** (claims inherited from research, not yet confirmed):
- A digital-tipping deployment reportedly live at **Sasol** service stations —
  identify the provider before pilot-site selection (see `15-competitive-lessons.md`).
- **~140,000** people employed across SA filling stations — directional only.
- Tappy's claimed 50k NFC devices / SARB approval — flagged in `15-competitive-lessons.md`.

## 1. Strategic principle

Worker-led, venue-enabled, customer-activated. The three audiences adopt for
different reasons:

- **Worker:** "I lose tips because customers do not carry cash."
- **Customer:** "I want to tip, but it must be quick, safe and trustworthy."
- **Employer/venue:** "I want to support my workers without administrative or
  financial complexity."

The worker remains the centre of the proposition. Do not market SwiftTip as a
payroll platform, performance system, or complex fintech. The opening proposition:

> **Scan. Tip. Done. The worker receives 100% of your tip.**

Digital tipping already exists in SA fuel retail (see Sasol verify-flag), so
SwiftTip cannot rely on novelty: differentiate on worker trust, operational
reliability, simple adoption, and proof of earnings impact.

## GTM-0 — Prepare the market (before the first live pilot)

**Objective:** enough trust, clarity and operational readiness to run a credible
pilot. Not a public launch.

**Audiences:** pilot-site owner/operator; ~20 participating workers; site
supervisors and cashiers; a small circle of potential follow-on venue owners.

**Positioning:**
- Employer: *Help your team receive the tips customers already want to give —
  without handling cash or running tip administration.*
- Worker: *Your profile. Your tips. Your money.*
- Customer: *No cash? You can still say thank you.*

**Required assets before pilot:** one-page employer proposition · worker
onboarding guide · customer "How to tip" visual · worker QR badges · pump,
till-point and entrance signage · pilot landing page · FAQ (safety, fees, payouts,
privacy) · plain-English "100% goes to the worker" explanation · venue staff
briefing pack · pilot measurement dashboard · basic media & incident-response plan.

**Critical trust messages (customer must immediately understand):**
1. The worker is verified. 2. The QR identifies the correct worker. 3. No customer
account required. 4. The worker receives the full stated tip. 5. Any processing
charge is displayed before payment. 6. Payment is secure. 7. The venue supports
the programme but does not take the worker's money.

Security, POPIA work and reconciliation should appear as *visible trust features*
in the customer experience — only once the underlying compliance work is genuinely
done (no unearned claims; see the launcher-footer rule in the checklist).

**GTM-0 exit gate — do not begin the pilot until:**
- Workers understand the product and voluntarily participate.
- The funds model is approved (`08-funds-model-decision.md`).
- Live payments and payouts tested end to end.
- **QR codes cannot easily be swapped or misassigned** (tip-page photo prominence
  is a security control; badges tamper-evident — see checklist).
- Support and escalation channels operational.
- The venue has an internal champion.
- **Baseline worker tip earnings recorded** (before/after depends on before).

## GTM-1 — Prove the tip (one fuel-station pilot)

**Objective:** prove real customers scan, pay and complete a tip — and workers
receive and value the income.

**Model: hyperlocal, assisted launch.** SwiftTip personnel visibly present during
launch: help workers present badges naturally; explain to customers without
pressure; observe hesitation points; test signage placement and wording; record
technical difficulties; resolve failed transactions immediately; gather feedback.

**Desired customer journey:** service completed → customer notices badge or is
politely informed → scans → verified identity appears → selects amount → sees
total and any processing cost → pays → confirmation → worker notified (without
unnecessary customer information).

**Pilot marketing = on-site only:** worker-worn QR, pump signage, windscreen
reminders, counter cards, till-slip prompts, launch-day demo, WhatsApp-ready venue
videos, local community posts, simple landing page. **No mass advertising** —
national impressions are worthless at one site.

**Behavioural message — lead with the human action, not the technology:**
> *Great service deserves an easy thank you.*
> *Scan the worker's verified SwiftTip code and tip securely — no cash or app required.*

**Metrics.**
*Customer funnel:* service interactions · QR scans · scan→payment conversion ·
payment success rate · median completion time · average/median tip · consented
repeat tipping · abandonment point.
*Worker outcomes:* activated workers · workers with ≥1 tip · tips per worker ·
incremental weekly earnings · tip distribution across workers · payout success ·
payout timing · worker satisfaction.
*Employer outcomes:* onboarding effort · support incidents · supervisor time ·
complaints · participation · disruption · willingness to continue or pay.

**Evidence to capture (the pilot's real output is a case study, not publicity):**
before/after earnings · total tips processed · payment success · worker, customer
and venue testimony · consented images/video · operational lessons · exact
abandonment reasons · effects on workers' lives *without exploiting their
circumstances*.

**Communications rule:** no dramatic national ambitions before the product works
consistently. The message: *SwiftTip is piloting a new way for South Africans to
tip verified service workers without cash.*

**GTM-1 exit gate:** reliable payment completion · accurate reconciliation ·
reliable payouts · meaningful worker participation · positive incremental earnings
· low venue burden · a credible case for sites two and three · no unresolved trust
or compliance failure.

## GTM-2 — Prove repeatability (3–10 sites)

**Objective:** prove the pilot wasn't an exception and launches don't require
founders managing every interaction.

**Stay in fuel retail.** Do not expand across restaurants, hotels, salons, car
guards, delivery and tourism yet — each has different workflows and tipping
behaviour. Beachhead targets: independent operators (1–5 sites); dealer groups;
stations with visible owner involvement; strong traffic and stable teams;
operators willing to share anonymised data.

**Geography: cluster model.** Gqeberha cluster first → East London → wider Eastern
Cape reference base → Gauteng only once launches are repeatable. Clusters buy
faster support, lower travel cost, word of mouth, retailer references, and
customer familiarity across sites.

**Sales motion (founder-led, relationship-led):** identify operator → warm
introduction → present pilot case study → site assessment → worker sample
interviews → agree measurable launch objectives → structured onboarding → 30-day
review → ask for a referral.

**Sell outcomes, not features:** workers' access to gratuities · morale and
retention · modern customer experience · less cash reliance · measurable social
impact · no employer handling of tips · visible worker-support initiative at low
operational cost.

**Commercial model (test three packages):** *Founding Site* (limited-period pilot
for data + reference) · *Standard Site* (monthly fee: onboarding, worker
management, reporting, support, QR materials) · *Multi-Site Partner* (group
agreement, central reporting). **Do not stay free forever** — free validates
interest, not willingness to pay.

**Demand generation:** direct outreach · owner referrals · fuel-retailer
associations · industry events · operator-aimed LinkedIn · local business media ·
case-study webinars · referral incentives · worker-led introductions (handled so
workers are never unpaid sales agents).

**GTM-2 exit gate:** several successful live sites · repeatable onboarding docs ·
predictable conversion ranges · ≥1 paying employer · ≥1 multi-site opportunity ·
strong worker retention · support not founder-dependent · clear site economics.

## GTM-3 — Win the fuel-retail beachhead (10–100 sites)

**Objective:** become the recognised, trusted digital-tipping platform for SA
fuel-station workers. Founder-led selling becomes a repeatable B2B2C engine.

**Three routes to market:** direct sales (independents, small groups) · strategic
accounts (regional groups, oil-company networks, national brands) · channel
partners (forecourt tech, POS, workforce platforms, consultants, payment/banking
partners, associations, signage suppliers) — pursued *only* where they reduce
acquisition/activation cost. A partnership announcement without live volume is not
distribution.

**Category to own:** *the worker-first digital tipping platform.* Proof points:
verified workers · 100% of stated tip · no customer app · transparent processing
cost · accurate reconciliation · worker-owned identity and records · employer
tools without worker surveillance.

**Brand architecture — one promise, three voices:**
- Customer: *See good service. Send a good tip.*
- Worker: *Your work. Your profile. Your tips.*
- Employer: *Support your team without handling their tips.*

**PR follows evidence:** total tips delivered · active workers · participating
sites · verified earnings uplift · new-province expansion · first major
dealer-group agreement · payout-reliability milestones · consented aggregate
financial-wellness findings. **Never use poverty as the marketing device** —
celebrate service, recognition and economic agency.

**Customer awareness (now appropriate, selectively):** forecourt media ·
geo-targeted social around live stations · brand search · everyday-service creator
partnerships · road-trip/motoring content · community radio in live markets ·
appreciation-based customer challenges · employer-funded launches.

**GTM-3 exit gate:** strong site retention · predictable customer usage ·
improving acquisition economics · sustainable employer revenue · recognised fuel
position · demand from ≥2 adjacent sectors · operational readiness to expand.

## GTM-4 — Expand by vertical

**Sequence:** 1. Hotels/accommodation (workers separated from the payment moment) ·
2. Restaurants/hospitality groups (large but complex: POS flows, pooling) ·
3. Tourism/experiences (guides, game-drive staff, shuttle drivers; cashless-native
international customers) · 4. Personal services (stylists, barbers, mobile
providers) · 5. Informal/independent workers (car guards — high need, hardest
onboarding/verification/payout/support).

**Vertical entry rule — enter only when you can answer:** who pays for employer
tooling · who owns the venue relationship · individual vs pooled tipping · how
workers are verified · dispute handling · QR display · the customer trigger moment
· required integration · profitable acquisition · does the model preserve worker
ownership.

Each vertical gets its own playbook (journey, sales narrative, onboarding,
signage, pooling rules, metrics, case study, pricing). **No generic "SwiftTip for
everyone" campaign.**

**GTM-4 exit gate (per vertical):** repeatable acquisition channel · strong worker
activation · healthy conversion · employer willingness to pay · acceptable support
cost · reference clients · positive retention.

## GTM-5 — Own the category (worker identity & earnings)

Narrative evolves: *Tip without cash* → *Recognise the person behind the service*
→ *A trusted earnings identity that belongs to the worker.*

**Worker-led growth loop:** worker joins at one venue → builds verified profile
and earnings history → changes employer → profile travels → new employer
encounters SwiftTip through the worker → another venue joins. This stays **worker
empowerment, never an obligation to recruit**.

**Programmes:** fairly-compensated worker ambassadors · verified worker stories ·
appreciation campaigns · skills and earnings education · training-institution
partnerships · financial-wellness content · employer recognition for responsible
adoption · annual service-worker earnings & appreciation report · a SwiftTip Trust
Mark for compliant venues.

**Enterprise/institutional GTM:** national hospitality groups · fuel brands ·
retail groups · tourism operators · airports/transport hubs · stadiums/events ·
corporate ESG programmes. Proposition: *a trusted system for recognising, paying
and understanding frontline service workers — without taking ownership away from
them.*

## 2. The two funnels

**Employer funnel:** target account → qualified conversation → site assessment →
pilot agreement → launch → 30-day review → paid continuation → multi-site →
referral. *Measures:* outreach→meeting · meeting→pilot · pilot→paid · time to
launch · monthly retention · site→multi-site · CAC · LTV.

**Customer funnel:** service moment → QR noticed → scanned → worker verified →
amount selected → payment initiated → completed → tip delivered. *Measures:* QR
visibility · scan rate · scan→payment · completion · time to complete · average
tip · consented repeats.

**Diagnosis rule:** a weak tip rate may mean poor signage, low awareness, an
awkward worker script, slow page load, or missing trust cues — **it is not
automatically lack of demand.** Diagnose site by site before concluding anything
about the north-star question.

## 3. Team by stage

**GTM-1:** founder/product lead · site activation lead · support & reconciliation
· part-time content/design. **GTM-2:** founder-led sales · implementation manager
· customer success · field agents where justified · analytics capability.
**GTM-3:** head of commercial · BDRs · strategic-account lead · CS team · regional
activation · growth marketing · comms/partnerships. **Hiring follows transaction
and site growth** — no national sales org before a repeatable motion.

## 4. Budget logic

**Stage A — evidence creation:** pilot materials, worker activation, research,
analytics, site support, content capture, case-study production. **Stage B —
account acquisition:** targeted B2B outreach, industry relationships, demos,
qualified events, case-study distribution, referrals, onboarding capability.
**Stage C — category demand** (only after substantial availability): consumer
campaigns, national PR, creators, broader media, sponsorships. Rule: **do not
create demand where customers cannot use the product.**

## 5. Growth flywheel

More participating workers → more tipping opportunities → more successful tips →
stronger earnings evidence → stronger employer case studies → more venues →
greater customer familiarity → more workers. Trust powers every stage: verified
identity → customer confidence → higher completion → more earnings → worker
advocacy → employer adoption.

## 6. First 12 months

**M1–2 Prepare:** finalise venue · baseline worker earnings · complete funds-model
decision · site materials · train workers/supervisors · analytics & support · test
the full customer and payout journey. **M3–4 Pilot:** launch one station ·
high-touch onsite support · observe behaviour · fix friction *without expanding
scope* · weekly reports · consented stories. **M5–6 Validate:** pilot evaluation ·
controlled case study · decide whether the proposition is proven · refine pricing
· recruit sites 2–3. **M7–9 Repeat:** 3–5 sites · test standard onboarding ·
measure cross-site differences · first paying employer · structured referrals.
**M10–12 Beachhead:** 5–10 strong sites (not many weak ones) · dealer-group
pipeline · dedicated activation lead · first impact report · decide on regional
expansion.

## 7. Decisions to settle before public marketing

1. Exact processing-fee presentation. 2. Meaning and evidence behind "100% goes to
the worker." 3. Who bears chargebacks/reversals (→ specialist brief). 4. Payout
schedule. 5. Min/max tip amounts. 6. How verification is communicated. 7. QR
replacement and fraud handling. 8. **Employer access to individual tip data:
decided — NO; aggregates only** (per `16-vision.md` standing rejections). 9. Pilot
employer pricing/concession. 10. Customer-support promise. 11. Worker-consent
process for testimonials. 12. Conditions for stopping or extending the pilot.

## 8. Core recommendation

Wedge-and-expand: **wedge** = petrol attendants at a few carefully selected
stations · **proof** = measurable incremental earnings, reliable payouts, easy
venue operations · **expansion** = independents → dealer groups → regional
networks · **adjacency** = one vertical at a time · **moat** = portable worker
identity and earnings history · **end state** = trusted infrastructure for
service-worker earnings.

The strongest launch story is not *"SwiftTip has built a digital tipping app."*
It is: *"At this station, SwiftTip helped 20 verified workers receive money
customers wanted to give them, delivered every tip accurately, and increased
worker earnings without creating administrative work for the employer."*
